“Hey, look! The old man is so slow he isn’t even registering our jokes!”

This time it is the man with countless rings on his nasty hands.

The smith returns to his work, taking another piece of steel into the raging fire.

As the burly men leave, they notice Shun sweeping in the corner.

“Hey, boy!”

“Why you working for this blankie?”

“Didn’t your mommy tell you these guys are freaks?”

Shun doesn’t even look their way.

The bearded man grabs one of the swords on display.

“Just look at how terrible these swords are!”

“I bet even you could make a better one, kid!”

Shun’s mouth opens in reply, but looking at the smith, he closes it.

“I would take the swords as a living fee, but they’re so shitty I’d rather keep my dignity!”

The man throws the sword on the ground.

The group leaves loudly.

Shun watches the smith, but he acts as if it never happened, even disregarding the thrown sword.

Shun picks it up. Although it was quite a powerful throw, the sword is unchanged, and the edge is still as sharp. The blade is simple, balanced perfectly on the cross guard, with a strict consistency throughout, and the pieces are perfectly fitted to each other.

“I could never make a sword like this at all!”

Shun looks to the man curiously.

“How can he just ignore all of this?!”

Shun carefully puts the sword back on its stand.

“But I can definitely learn a lot about smithing from him…”

The grey-haired man nods towards the bellows, as the fire has started to quieten down.

Leaving the splintered broom in the corner, Shun starts to press down on the bellows, but it is much tougher than he expected.

After nonstop blowing, Shun looks up at the man for confirmation, but the man doesn’t even look back.

Looking into the now raging fire, he sees steel shift from dull red to orange to yellow, and as it starts to turn white, the smith takes it out with tongs and onto his exhausted anvil.

And after almost ten minutes, Shun finally sees the man nod.

“Get some water from the well to fill up the barrel.”

Confused, Shun is about to open his mouth—

“There’s one on the street on the right.”

“Take the buckets from behind the barrel.”

Shun, already tired from half a day’s worth of work, grabs the buckets and goes on his way.

Walking through the granite and slate houses, Shun enjoys some of the sun and the more comfortable roads.

“Pff, look at his bucket.”

“Even blankies can get slaves.”

A boy barely older than Shun sneers to his friends.

Their leather armor is dirty, grime visible from every angle, their swords chipped.

A girl with a scar on her right arm remarks, “Imagine working for a blank.”

The four of them snigger. Shun catches their eyes, and they turn their heads the other way.

The well isn’t terribly busy, and with the chilly winter coming, most are getting firewood.

Putting his bucket on the well, Shun finds cranking the shaft easy compared to the bellows.

And shortly, with two full buckets, Shun walks back to the wooden houses, saying goodbye to the well-kept roads.

“I would kill for some running shoes.”

Back at the smith’s, Shun dumps both buckets in.

And the morning blurs into noon. As Shun chucks some charcoal into the furnace, the door opens quietly and four teenagers enter, leather armor stained with grime and the blood of who knows what. The girl walking up with a chipped sword has a scar on her right arm. For a split second they share eye contact, but she quickly turns to the smith.

Muttering, “What’s your cheapest sword?”

The rest look away, silent.

Shun grips the shovel in his hand tightly.

“Those over there are fifty flaions each.” The man points to a barrel filled with swords of all variations.

She grabs four swords from the barrel and, without once looking the man in the eyes, gives him forty silver coins. She and the rest walk off quickly before they can be spotted.

“Those damn hypocrites!”

Shun finally breaks after all the tension.

“How can you just stay silent all the time!?”

“These people mock you and your craft day and night, yet you can’t even defend yourself! All you do is just…”

“Just…”

The fire rages and crackles on with the new supply of coal.

The man looks properly at Shun’s face for the first time. “As long as I can still hammer away, it doesn’t matter.”

“And as long as they buy, I can keep doing the craft I devoted my life to.”

Shun, flustered, just works on the furnace silently.

“Being ‘devoted’ doesn’t do any good.”

Shun keeps working quietly, commanded only by body language from the smith.

Finally, the sun sets and twilight comes.

The man hands him his pay.

It doesn’t weigh much, and looking inside the small bag, he sees ten silver coins.

Looking back, he sees the smith’s straight face curve up for the first time in what seems to be a while.

His stomach rumbles after the long day at work but with nowhere to get food from Shun pushes through

And guided only by the last sliver of sunlight, Shun goes back to his old, abandoned shelter.
